1. Units & Systems of Measurement

Characteristics of a Unit

The chosen standard used for measuring a physical quantity is called a unit. It should be: well defined, easy to reproduce, easy to compare, internationally accepted, and independent of changes in physical conditions.

Unit Types: Fundamental Units and Derived Units. Internationally accepted systems include CGS, MKS, FPS, and SI Units.

Seven Fundamental SI Units

  • Length: metre (M)
  • Temperature: kelvin (K)
  • Mass: kilogram (Kg)
  • Luminous intensity: candela (Cd)
  • Time: second (S)
  • Amount of substance: mole (Mol)
  • Electric Current: ampere (A)

Supplementary units: radian [rad] for plane angle and steradian [sr] for solid angle.

3. Inventions & Discoveries in Physics

Key Inventions

  • Aeroplane: Wright brothers (USA, 1903)
  • Barometer: E. Torricelli (Italy, 1644)
  • Cinematograph: Thomas Alva Edison (USA, 1891)
  • Printing press: J. Gutenberg (Germany, 1455)
  • Radar: Dr A.H. Taylor and L.C. Young (USA, 1922)
  • Telephone: Alexander Graham Bell (USA, 1876)
  • Television: John Logie Baird (Scotland, 1926)
  • X-ray: Wilhelm Roentgen (Germany, 1895)

Key Discoveries

  • Laws of motion: Newton (1687)
  • Atom: John Dalton (1808)
  • Electromagnetic Induction: Michael Faraday (1831)
  • Radioactivity: Henry Becquerel (1896)
  • Electron: J.J. Thomson (1897)
  • Photoelectric effect & Relativity: Albert Einstein (1905)
  • Proton: Rutherford (1919)
  • Neutron: James Chadwick (1932)
